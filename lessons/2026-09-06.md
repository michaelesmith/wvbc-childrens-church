# The Prodigal Son — “God’s Forgiving Love” #

**Memory Verse:** “‘For this son of mine was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found.’” — Luke 15:24

### Scripture — Luke 15:11–32 (NIV)

### Object Lesson (5 min) — “The Muddy Coat”

*You’ll need: an old, dirty coat or shirt (rub some dirt or make it wrinkled and messy), and a clean, nice robe or jacket.*

Hold up the dirty coat. Say: “This is what the son looked like when he came home. He’d been feeding pigs, sleeping outside, out of money, out of everything. Head down. Ashamed.”

Ask the kids: “If YOU were the dad, and your kid ran off and wasted everything, and now they’re walking up the driveway looking like this — what would you do?” Let them answer honestly.

Then say: “God’s answer is amazing. He didn’t say ‘I told you so.’ He didn’t make him stand outside. He RAN to him. He hugged him even though he was still dirty. Then…”

Swap the dirty coat for the clean robe. “He put the BEST robe on him. He gave him a ring. He threw a PARTY. That’s how God feels when we come home.”

### Discussion Questions

- What did the younger son ask for? What did he do with it?

- What was he doing when he “came to his senses”? (Feeding pigs!)

- What did he plan to say to his father?

- How did the father react when he saw him coming?

- Why was the older brother upset? Was he wrong?

- Who does the father in this story represent? (God the Father.)

- Have you ever felt far from God? What did you do?

### Activity (8 min) — Story Retell with Roles

Assign roles: Younger Son, Father, Older Son, Pigs, Servants.

Walk through the story slowly, with narration from the leader.

Highlight the moments: getting the money, spending it, ending up with pigs, the long walk home, the father RUNNING out, the robe/ring/sandals, the party, the older brother’s complaint.

Ask each “character” at the end: “What did you feel?”

### Craft (5 min) — “Welcome Home” Banner

Give each child a piece of paper folded into a banner.

On the front: “WELCOME HOME” in colorful letters.

Underneath: draw a robe, a ring, and sandals — the father’s three gifts.

On the back write: “God always runs to me when I come home.”

### Game (5 min) — “Come Home” Race

Set a “far country” at one end of the room and a “home” at the other.

Kids start in the far country. On “GO!” they race home.

When they arrive, the leader (as the father) hugs them and says, “WELCOME HOME!”

Everyone always wins. Everyone always gets welcomed. Just like God.

### Takeaways

- No matter how far we run, God is watching for us to come home.

- God’s love isn’t earned — it’s freely given.

- Coming home to God means being celebrated, not shamed.

- There’s a warning for “older brothers” too: don’t be resentful when God is generous.
