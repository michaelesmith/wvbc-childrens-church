# The Paralyzed Man and His Friends — “Faithful Friends” #

**Memory Verse:** “When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralyzed man, ‘Son, your sins are forgiven.’” — Mark 2:5 (NIV)

### Scripture — Mark 2:1–12 (NIV)

¹ A few days later, when Jesus again entered Capernaum, the people heard that he had come home. ² They gathered in such large numbers that there was no room left, not even outside the door, and he preached the word to them.

³ Some men came, bringing to him a paralyzed man, carried by four of them. ⁴ Since they could not get him to Jesus because of the crowd, they made an opening in the roof above Jesus by digging through it and then lowered the mat the man was lying on.

⁵ When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralyzed man, “Son, your sins are forgiven.”

⁶ Now some teachers of the law were sitting there, thinking to themselves, ⁷ “Why does this fellow talk like that? He’s blaspheming! Who can forgive sins but God alone?”

⁸ Immediately Jesus knew in his spirit that this was what they were thinking in their hearts, and he said to them, “Why are you thinking these things? ⁹ Which is easier: to say to this paralyzed man, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Get up, take your mat and walk’? ¹⁰ But I want you to know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins.” So he said to the man, ¹¹ “I tell you, get up, take your mat and go home.”

¹² He got up, took his mat and walked out in full view of them all. This amazed everyone and they praised God, saying, “We have never seen anything like this!”

### Object Lesson (5 min) — “Four Corners of a Blanket”

*You’ll need: a small blanket or towel and a stuffed animal.*

Place the stuffed animal on the blanket. Say: “This is our friend. He can’t walk. He needs help getting to Jesus. Can just ONE person carry him?” Try lifting one corner — the animal slides.

Ask for FOUR volunteers. Have each grab a corner. Together they lift and walk it across the room to a “Jesus” spot.

Say: “Faithful friends do TOGETHER what one person can’t do alone. Those four friends didn’t just say ‘I hope Jesus heals him.’ They CARRIED him. And when the door was blocked, they went through the ROOF.”

End: “Who in your life needs a faithful friend to help them get to Jesus? Could you be that friend?”

### Discussion Questions

- What was wrong with the man in the story?

- How many friends carried him? How did they get to Jesus when the house was full?

- What did Jesus do FIRST — heal his body or forgive his sins? Why is that surprising?

- Why were the teachers of the law upset?

- What did Jesus prove by healing the man?

- Who are your faithful friends? Who could YOU carry to Jesus (in prayer, an invitation, or a kind act)?

### Activity (8 min) — “Team Lift”

Break into groups of four. Give each group a blanket or sheet and a stuffed animal (or basketball).

Each group must carry their “friend” across the room to “Jesus” without dropping.

Add obstacles: chairs to go around, a low doorway to duck under.

First team to safely deliver their friend to Jesus wins. Talk after: “It takes coordination and TRUST to carry a friend to Jesus.”

### Craft (5 min) — “Four Friends” Handprint Frame

Give each child a piece of paper with a small paper mat in the middle.

They stamp four handprints (or trace four hands) around the mat — one at each corner — to represent the four friends.

On the mat, write: “Who can I carry to Jesus?”

Write names of friends/family they want to pray for on each finger.

### Game (5 min) — “Roof Relay”

Stack paper cups into a small “roof” on a table.

Under each cup, hide a paper “friend” name.

Kids take turns running up, knocking down a cup, grabbing the paper, and running back.

At the end, share the names — that’s who we’re going to “carry” to Jesus in prayer this week.

### Takeaways

- Faithful friends bring each other to Jesus — no matter what it takes.

- Jesus cares about our hearts AND our bodies. Forgiveness first, healing second.

- Be the kind of friend who lifts others toward Jesus.
