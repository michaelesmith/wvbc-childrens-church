# Manna in the Wilderness — “Thankful, Not Grumbling” #

**Memory Verse:** “Do everything without grumbling or arguing.” — Philippians 2:14 (NIV)

### Theme Specific

#### Thankful Leaves

Each child adds a leaf to the Thankful Wall for an everyday gift we usually take for granted — food, a home, family, clean water, a warm bed.

### Object Lesson (5 min) — “Grumble Glasses”

*You’ll need: a pair of dark sunglasses (the “grumble glasses”) and a pair of bright or silly glasses (the “thankful glasses”).*

Put on the dark sunglasses and look around the room. Grumble out loud: “Ugh, it’s so dark in here. This chair is lumpy. I didn’t get the snack I wanted.”

Ask: “Did the room change, or did my glasses change?” Let the kids answer.

Swap to the bright glasses and look around again: “Wow, I have a chair to sit on! I have friends here! I get to learn about God today!”

Say: “The Israelites had just watched God part the Red Sea. But when they got hungry, they put on their grumble glasses and forgot everything God had done. God still gave them bread from heaven every single morning. When we put on thankful glasses, we see how much God is already taking care of us.”

### Discussion Questions

- What amazing thing had God just done for the Israelites before this story? (Rescued them from Egypt, parted the Red Sea.)

- What did the people grumble about? What did they say about Egypt?

- Moses said their grumbling was really against whom? (v. 8 — the Lord.)

- How did God answer their grumbling? (Quail in the evening, manna in the morning.)

- Why do you think the Israelites forgot what God had already done for them?

- What is something you have every day that you might forget to thank God for?

- What can you do the next time you feel like grumbling?

### Activity (8 min) — Grumble or Thanks?

Read short everyday situations out loud:

- “Mom made a dinner you don’t like.”
- “It’s raining on game day.”
- “Your sister got the bigger cookie.”
- “You have to clean your room before you can play.”
- “Your friend can’t come over today.”

For each one, kids give a thumbs-down and say a grumbly answer, then a thumbs-up and say what the thankful answer would be.

Talk about it: the situation didn’t change, but our hearts did.

### Craft (5 min) — Grumble Jar to Thankful Jar

Give each child a paper cup or a printed jar outline.

On one side they write “Grumble Jar” and on the other side “Thankful Jar.” Decorate with markers and stickers.

Explain the take-home challenge: every time someone in the family grumbles this week, they trade it for something they’re thankful for and drop a slip of paper in the jar.

### Game (5 min) — Manna Collect

Scatter mini marshmallows or Cheerios on a clean sheet or table — it’s “morning” in the desert.

Give each child one small cup. They gather just enough to fill their cup — no more, no less.

Check cups: just like the Israelites, everyone has exactly what they need (v. 18).

Kids may eat their manna when the game is done.

### Takeaways

- God takes care of what we need every day — our “daily bread.”

- Grumbling forgets what God has done. Thankfulness remembers.

- Even when His people complained, God was kind and gave anyway.

- A thankful heart trusts God instead of grumbling.

### Put It in Practice

Use the Grumble Jar at home — every grumble gets traded for something you’re thankful for.
