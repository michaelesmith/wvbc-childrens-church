# Jesus is the Powerful Son of God — Jesus Performed Miracles #

**Memory Verse:** “<u>The people were amazed</u> when they saw the mute speaking, the crippled made well, the lame walking and the blind seeing. <u>And they praised the God of Israel.</u>” — Matthew 15:31 (NIV)

### Scripture — Matthew 15:29–39 (NIV)

²⁹ Jesus left there and went along the Sea of Galilee. Then he went up on a mountainside and sat down. ³⁰ Great crowds came to him, bringing the lame, the blind, the crippled, the mute and many others, and laid them at his feet; and he healed them. ³¹ The people were amazed when they saw the mute speaking, the crippled made well, the lame walking and the blind seeing. And they praised the God of Israel.

³⁵ He told the crowd to sit down on the ground. ³⁶ Then he took the seven loaves and the fish, and when he had given thanks, he broke them and gave them to the disciples, and they in turn to the people. ³⁷ They all ate and were satisfied. Afterward the disciples picked up seven basketfuls of broken pieces that were left over. ³⁸ The number of those who ate was four thousand men, besides women and children.

³⁹ After Jesus had sent the crowd away, he got into the boat and went to the vicinity of Magadan.

### Object Lesson (5 min) — “Seven Loaves, One Big Problem”

*You’ll need: 7 dinner rolls (or 7 slices of bread), a couple of small toy fish or fish crackers, a large basket, and a picture or list showing “4,000+ people.”*

Set the basket on a table. Hold up the 4,000 people picture and ask: “If you had to feed ALL of these people—moms, dads, kids, grandparents—using only this…” (drop the 7 rolls and the fish into the basket), “…how far would it go?”

Let the kids laugh and guess. Maybe one person, maybe a family. Definitely not thousands.

Say: “The disciples had the exact same problem. They looked at what they had, looked at the huge crowd, and said, ‘It’s impossible.’ But when Jesus took it, thanked God, and started breaking the bread, it didn’t run out. Everyone ate until they were FULL, and there were SEVEN baskets left over.”

Hold the basket up and say: “Miracles happen when we put what we have—no matter how small—into the hands of Jesus. He is the powerful Son of God.”

### Discussion Questions

- What kinds of miracles did Jesus do in this passage? (Healings + feeding.)

- Why do you think Jesus wanted to feed the crowd? (Verse 32—He had compassion.)

- Have you ever felt like what you had was too small for what was needed?

- What does it teach us about Jesus when He uses 7 loaves to feed thousands?

- Why do miracles matter? What do they show about who Jesus is?

- Can Jesus still do powerful things today? How?

### Activity (8 min) — Miracle Sort

Print or write out several miracle stories of Jesus on cards: calmed the storm, walked on water, healed the blind man, raised Lazarus, fed 5,000, fed 4,000, turned water into wine, healed a paralyzed man, healed the woman who touched His robe.

Lay the cards face down. Kids take turns flipping a card, reading it aloud, and sorting it into categories: “Power Over Nature,” “Power Over Sickness,” “Power Over Death,” “Power to Provide.”

Discuss: Jesus has power over everything—because He is the powerful Son of God.

### Craft (5 min) — Basket of Loaves Count-and-Color

Give each child a printed picture of a basket with bread rolls inside.

On each roll, they write one word describing Jesus’ power (powerful, kind, loving, healer, provider, Savior, miracle-worker).

Color the basket and write at the top: “Jesus Has Power Over Everything.”

Kids take home as a reminder of the miracle.

### Game (5 min) — “Pass the Basket” Memory Challenge

Kids stand in a circle with a basket. Play music.

When the music stops, the child holding the basket has to name one miracle Jesus did.

No repeats. If they can’t name one, they sit down and cheer others on. Last one standing wins.

Great way to reinforce how MANY miracles Jesus performed.

### Takeaways

- Jesus is the powerful Son of God—His miracles prove He is truly God.

- Jesus cares about people’s real, everyday needs: hunger, sickness, fear.

- When we bring what little we have to Jesus, He can do far more than we imagine.
