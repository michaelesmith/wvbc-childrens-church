# The Promised King — “God Keeps His Promises” #

**Memory Verse:** “For to us a child is born, to us a son is given, and the government will be on his shoulders. And he will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.” — Isaiah 9:6 (NIV)

### Theme Specific

#### Advent Wreath — Hope and Peace

Set up the tabletop wreath with four candles (three purple, one pink) around a white center candle. Explain: “Advent means *coming*. For four Sundays before Christmas, we light one candle at a time while we get ready to celebrate Jesus coming.”

We’re starting on the second Sunday, so light two today. One child lights the first purple candle (**Hope**): God’s people waited a long time, hoping for the Savior He promised. A second child lights the next purple candle (**Peace**): Isaiah called Him the “Prince of Peace.”

Say the memory verse together while the candles are lit.

#### Suggestions (to decide later)

- **Promise ornaments:** Add one ornament to a small tabletop tree each week. This week: a **crown** for the promised King (Isaiah 9:6–7). Start the tree with this first ornament.
- **Journey to Bethlehem:** Use a nativity set where the figures move closer to the manger each week. This week: Set up the stable with the manger empty. Mary and Joseph start on the far side of the room, still on their way. Talk about how God’s people had to wait a long time for the promised King.

### Object Lesson (5 min) — “A Light in the Dark”

*You’ll need: a dark room, one small candle or LED tea light, and a flashlight.*

Turn off all the lights. Ask: “How does it feel when it’s really dark? Can you see where you’re going?”

Say: “For hundreds of years, God’s people felt like they were walking in the dark. They were waiting for the King God had promised. About 700 years before Jesus was born, God gave the prophet Isaiah a message: ‘The people walking in darkness have seen a great light.’”

Turn on the one small light. Say: “Look how much ONE little light changes the whole room! Isaiah said that light would come as a baby — a child born to us. That baby is Jesus.”

End: “God’s people waited a LONG time. But God always keeps His promises. Jesus came, right on time.”

### Discussion Questions

- What does it feel like to wait for something you really want?

- Isaiah wrote this promise about 700 years before Jesus was born. Why do you think God’s people kept waiting and hoping?

- Isaiah said the people were walking in darkness. What kind of “darkness” do people feel today? (Sad, scared, lonely, doing wrong.)

- What four names did Isaiah give the promised child? Which one is your favorite, and why?

- What does it mean that Jesus is the “Prince of Peace”?

- God kept His promise to send Jesus. What other promises has God made to us?

### Activity (8 min) — Names of the King

Write the four names from Isaiah 9:6 on large cards: **Wonderful Counselor**, **Mighty God**, **Everlasting Father**, **Prince of Peace**. Put one in each corner of the room.

Talk through what each name means in kid language:

- **Wonderful Counselor** — He always gives the best advice.
- **Mighty God** — He is stronger than anything.
- **Everlasting Father** — He loves and cares for us forever.
- **Prince of Peace** — He makes things right between us and God, and calms our hearts.

Call out a situation (“You’re scared of a storm,” “You don’t know what to do about a friend,” “You feel all alone”). Kids run to the name of Jesus that fits best and explain why. There can be more than one right answer!

### Craft (5 min) — Promise Countdown Chain

Give each child paper strips (green and red) and a glue stick or stapler.

Kids make a paper chain with one link for each day until Christmas. On the top link, write “God keeps His promises!” (Isaiah 9:6).

At home, they tear off one link each day. The chain gets shorter as Christmas comes closer — just like God’s people counting down to the promised King.

### Game (5 min) — Light Tag

Dim the lights. One child holds a flashlight and stands in the middle.

Everyone else moves slowly around the room. When the flashlight beam lands on someone, they freeze and call out one of the four names from Isaiah 9:6.

That child then takes the flashlight. Keep going until everyone has had a turn.

### Takeaways

- God always keeps His promises — even when we have to wait.

- Jesus is the light God promised to people walking in darkness.

- Jesus is our Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, and Prince of Peace.

### Put It in Practice

Use your Promise Countdown Chain at home. Each night when you tear off a link, thank God for one promise He has kept.
