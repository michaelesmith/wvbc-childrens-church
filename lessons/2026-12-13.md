# Good News of Great Joy — “Go and Tell” #

**Memory Verse:** “But the angel said to them, ‘Do not be afraid. I bring you good news that will cause great joy for all the people. Today in the town of David a Savior has been born to you; he is the Messiah, the Lord.’” — Luke 2:10–11 (NIV)

### Theme Specific

#### Advent Wreath — Joy

Relight the two purple candles from last week (**Hope** and **Peace**) and review: “God promised a Savior, and Jesus is the Prince of Peace.”

One child lights the pink candle (**Joy**). Explain: “This candle is a different color because joy is different! It’s the happy, can’t-keep-it-in feeling the shepherds had when the angel told them Jesus was born.”

Say last week’s memory verse (Isaiah 9:6), then this week’s (Luke 2:10–11) while the candles are lit.

#### Suggestions (to decide later)

- **Promise ornaments:** Add one ornament to a small tabletop tree each week. This week: a **star** or **shepherd’s staff** for the good news the shepherds heard (Luke 2:8–20).
- **Journey to Bethlehem:** Use a nativity set where the figures move closer to the manger each week. This week: Move Mary and Joseph closer to the stable. Add the shepherds and sheep out in the “fields” at the edge of the scene, ready to hurry to Bethlehem.

### Object Lesson (5 min) — “Too Good to Keep Quiet”

*You’ll need: a confetti popper (or a handful of confetti in an envelope) and a rolled-up paper “megaphone.”*

Ask: “What’s the BEST news you’ve ever heard? Who did you tell first?” Let a few kids share.

Hold up the confetti popper. Say: “Good news is like this. When it’s really good, it just has to come out!” Pop it (or toss the confetti).

Say: “The shepherds were ordinary workers out in the fields at night. Nobody thought they were important. But God sent His angels to tell THEM first! When they saw baby Jesus, the joy was too big to hold in.”

Hold up the megaphone. Say: “They went and told everyone they could. And this good news is for ‘all the people’ — that includes you, and everyone you know.”

### Discussion Questions

- Who were the shepherds, and what were they doing when the angel came?

- How did the shepherds feel at first? What did the angel tell them? (v. 10)

- What sign did the angel give them to find Jesus? (v. 12)

- Why do you think God told shepherds first, instead of kings or important people?

- What did the shepherds do after they saw Jesus? (v. 17, 20)

- The angel said the good news is for “all the people.” Who is someone you could tell?

- What makes you feel joy at Christmas? Is it the presents, or something more?

### Activity (8 min) — Night in the Fields

Act out the story. Assign roles: shepherds, sheep, one angel, the heavenly host (angel choir), Mary, Joseph, and baby Jesus (a doll wrapped in a blanket in a box “manger”).

Dim the lights. Shepherds and sheep “sleep” in the field. The angel appears (flashlight on!) and says, “Do not be afraid! I bring you good news of great joy!”

The angel choir jumps up and shouts, “Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace!”

Shepherds hurry across the room to find the baby in the manger. Then they run back to the “town” and tell everyone else — the other kids, a leader, or even a parent at the door!

### Craft (5 min) — Good News Megaphone

Give each child a sheet of cardstock to roll into a cone and tape.

Kids decorate it with stars, angels, and sheep. Along the side write: “Good news of great joy!” (Luke 2:10)

They take it home and use it to “announce” the good news of Jesus to their family.

### Game (5 min) — Find the Sign

Hide a small baby doll wrapped in cloth (or a picture of baby Jesus in a manger) somewhere in the room while the kids cover their eyes.

Kids search like the shepherds looking for the sign. Whoever finds it shouts, “A Savior has been born!”

The finder hides it next. Play several rounds.

### Takeaways

- The good news of Jesus is for all people — even ordinary shepherds, and even us.

- Joy is what happens when we see that God kept His promise.

- The shepherds didn’t keep the good news to themselves; they went and told everyone.

- We can share the good news of Jesus too.

### Put It in Practice

Use your Good News Megaphone to tell one person this week that Jesus was born to be our Savior — a friend, a neighbor, or someone in your family.
